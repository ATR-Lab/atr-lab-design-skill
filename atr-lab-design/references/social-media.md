# Social media

How the Advanced Telerobotics Research (ATR) Lab at Kent State University runs its social channels: the
accounts that really exist, the profile kit and Kent State's required elements, sizes and safe zones for
every platform, what to post and how often, and ready recipes for the lab's recurring moments, plus
accessibility, consent, moderation and measurement.

**Read this when** you write or design any social post, carousel, video cut, avatar or banner for the lab,
set up or audit an account, or plan a posting calendar. For press, events, email and outreach, read
references/pr-events-outreach.md. For caption voice and house style, read references/voice-and-copy.md. The
canonical boilerplates and the social bio are in references/brand-foundation.md §10; if a copy here differs,
that section wins.

## 1. Six ground rules

1. **Use each platform's real handle and never print "@atr_kent".** It does not appear to exist (X,
   YouTube and TikTok return the same not-found response as a made-up control handle; the Instagram check was
   inconclusive, with no sign of lab use). It came from the old presentation template's footer. "ATR_Kent" is
   the competition-team name and the roundel text, not a handle (§2).
2. **Pair "ATR" with Kent State every time.** "ATR" alone also names a large robotics institute in Kyoto
   (atr.jp) that works on teleoperated robots. "ATR Lab" is fine in captions, handles and cards. In longer
   copy (LinkedIn, YouTube descriptions), write "Advanced Telerobotics Research Lab at Kent State
   University" on first mention (references/voice-and-copy.md).
3. **Every post points to something the lab owns**: a project page, a news post, the Join page or a
   YouTube video. Social posts last hours to a day; pages and videos keep working for years and are what
   search engines and AI assistants cite. Put the depth there and use posts as pointers.
4. **Robot content is honest.** Label playback speed ("2× speed"), say whether the robot is
   teleoperated, autonomous or scripted, and say "simulation" when it is. AI-generated images appear only as
   labeled illustrations, never as the lab's robots, people, experiments or results
   (references/ai-image-pipeline.md, references/imagery.md).
5. **Minors: a signed parent or guardian release, no names and no DMs** (§10, and
   references/kent-state-compliance.md).
6. **Alt text on every image and captions on every video.** Kent State's accessibility obligations cover
   new social posts (WCAG 2.1 AA under ADA Title II, deadline April 26, 2027); the lab's own floor is
   WCAG 2.2 AA (§9).

### 3.2 Kent State's required elements (every lab account)

Source: kent.edu/ucm/social/10-required-elements, the UCM social-media guide
(kent.edu/ucm/social/guide-social-media) and Policy 5-10.4
(kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-social-media-activity).

- [ ] **Handle and name** identify the lab (the unit), not Kent State as a whole. No duplicate accounts.
- [ ] **Category**: Higher Education, College and University, or Institution, whichever the platform offers.
- [ ] **Disclaimer** in the About section, **copied verbatim** from the required-elements page (it begins
      "Note: The views and opinions posted by visitors to this website do not reflect..."). Never paraphrase it.
- [ ] **Avatar** is the ATR mark (unit-specific), per §3.1.
- [ ] **Bio** says it is the official account of the lab and links to kent.edu and social.kent.edu.
- [ ] **Administrators**: at least one Kent State employee (the PI) **plus UCM's account**. Tell UCM when the
      account owner changes.
- [ ] **Accessibility** as in §9.
- [ ] **Content plan** (§6) and **KPIs** (§12) written down.
- [ ] **Directory listing**: email social@kent.edu to be listed. The lab is not listed yet; Computer Science is.
- [ ] **Content limits**: no personal opinions from the lab account; no endorsements, political or product
      promotion using the Kent State name; no copyrighted or trademarked material without permission;
      follow FERPA and HIPAA; post nothing confidential about students or staff.

### 3.3 Keeping accounts alive through student turnover

- UCM expects about **an hour a day** for an active account; plan the cadence in §6 around the real hours
  available.
- Name a **primary admin and a trained backup** each semester. When a student admin leaves, hand the account
  over formally. UCM warns that orphaned accounts may be deleted or flagged "no longer active".
- Give people **platform roles** (LinkedIn page admin, Meta Business roles, GitHub org roles) instead of
  sharing a password where the platform allows it. Where a shared login is unavoidable, the PI holds it and
  it is changed at every handover. Keep the recovery email on a kent.edu address the PI controls.
- Once a year: re-check this file's §2 table, the bio, the disclaimer text and the admin list.

## 5. Visual templates for posts

### 5.1 Where the templates live

- **PowerPoint templates (the default for designed cards):** `assets/templates/social/`, one file per format.
  Every file has the same eight named layouts (Announcement, Event, Paper accepted, Recruiting, Milestone,
  Spotlight quote, Demo video cover, Thank you / Welcome) and one showcase slide per layout. The hazard band,
  the constellation art and the logos sit on the layouts and follow §5.3, so type into the placeholders only.
  The one exception is the Demo video cover's play badge, which is a slide object: make a new cover by
  duplicating that showcase slide. Every text placeholder shrinks when the copy overflows; treat that as a
  sign to cut words, not as a fix. Slide inches are pixels ÷ 96, so a slide exports at exactly its pixel size.
  Editing, export steps per app and the full type scale are in that folder's `README.md`.

  | File | Pixels | Use it for |
  |---|---|---|
  | `ATR-Social-Square-1080.pptx` | 1080×1080 | LinkedIn and Facebook square posts, X image posts. Not for the Instagram feed: its 3:4 profile grid shows only the center 810 px of a square, which cuts the headline and both signatures. Use the Portrait file there |
  | `ATR-Social-Portrait-1080x1350.pptx` | 1080×1350 (4:5) | Instagram and Facebook feed (the default), LinkedIn image posts, Threads. Text and logos stay inside x 72–1008, so the 3:4 grid crop loses nothing |
  | `ATR-Social-Story-1080x1920.pptx` | 1080×1920 (9:16) | Stories, Reels covers, TikTok, Shorts covers. Text and logos stay inside x 72–940, y 288–1248, which clears the Meta and TikTok safe zones at once |
  | `ATR-Social-Landscape-1200x675.pptx` | 1200×675 (16:9) | X and LinkedIn image and link posts. The hazard band runs down the left edge, so the 2:1 and 1.91:1 link-card crops never cut it |
  | `ATR-YouTube-Thumbnail-1280x720.pptx` | 1280×720 (16:9) | YouTube thumbnails. Both signatures end at y 630, so nothing sits under the duration badge (x ≥ 1100, y ≥ 640). Export at 3840×2160 when the app allows it |

  The files set type in fixed pixels on every canvas: eyebrows 36 px; links, dates and honesty labels 36 to
  40 px; body 40 to 44 px; headlines 72 to 112 px; paper and demo-video titles 52 to 80 px. The 16:9 files
  use the same small sizes as the 1080 px files, which meets §5.3 because the floors hold in pixels up to
  1280 px wide.

- **Card renderer:** `scripts/social_card.py` (version 1.2.0; `--version`) builds a card PNG and an alt-text
  sidecar (`.txt`, same name) from a JSON spec. Setup (a venv with Pillow and numpy; fontTools is optional,
  for kerning) is in `scripts/README.md`, and the spec format is in the script's docstring. It follows §5.3
  (type floors, signature sizes and placement, band clearance and contrast), so its cards can be published like
  the PowerPoint templates'.
  - **Templates** (`--list-templates`), with the size and background each uses when the spec names none:
    `announcement` (square, navy), `event` (portrait, navy), `outreach` (portrait, gold: the K-12 and
    community-event card, §7.5), `publication` (square, navy), `recruiting` (square, gold), `stat` (square,
    navy), `quote` (square, navy), `video-cover` (youtube-thumb, navy) and `welcome` (square, gold). **Most
    default to `square`, which is not for the Instagram feed:** for Instagram, set `"size": "portrait"` in the
    spec or pass `--size portrait`. `event` and `outreach` take `date`, `time` and `place` rows (one line each;
    the time is a continuation row under the date, so write `"date": "June 14-18, 2027"`, `"time": "Mon.-Fri.,
    9 a.m.-3 p.m."`); `outreach` sets the headline up to 112 px and the rows at 44 px.
  - **Sizes** (`--list-sizes`; choose with `--size`, `--sizes` or `--all-sizes`). Every size is typeset at
    width ÷ 1080, because a 16:9 card fills the same phone column as a 1080 px tile. So the §5.3 floors and the
    signatures grow with the width, which makes the renderer stricter than the templates on 16:9 cards:

    | Size | Pixels | Margin (px) | Floors: any / supporting / headline (px) | ATR mark / Kent State wordmark (px) | Use it for |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | `square` | 1080×1080 | 72 | 36 / 40 / 64 | 130 tall / 136×130, bottom right | LinkedIn, Facebook and X square posts; not the Instagram feed (see the Square file above) |
    | `portrait` | 1080×1350 | 72 | 36 / 40 / 64 | 130 / 136×130, bottom right | Instagram and Facebook feed (the recommended feed size), LinkedIn and Threads |
    | `story` | 1080×1920 | 72 | 36 / 40 / 64 | 130 / 136×130, bottom right | Instagram and Facebook Stories and Reels covers; not TikTok (below) |
    | `landscape` | 1200×675 | 80 | 40 / 44 / 71 | 100 / 105×100, bottom right | 16:9 image posts on X, LinkedIn and Facebook, website news |
    | `linkedin` | 1200×627 | 80 | 40 / 44 / 71 | 100 / 105×100, bottom right | Link cards (1.91:1): LinkedIn link posts, X link cards |
    | `x-post` | 1600×900 | 107 | 53 / 59 / 95 | 133 / 139×133, bottom right | X image posts |
    | `youtube-thumb` | 1280×720 | 85 | 43 / 47 / 76 | 107 / 112×107, top right | YouTube thumbnails; the bottom-right 180×80 px (x ≥ 1100, y ≥ 640) stays empty for the duration badge |

    "Any" covers eyebrows, credits, dates, links and the CTA; "supporting" covers body text, bullets and labels;
    "headline" is the main line. A paper title (`publication`) is the exception to the headline floor: it can
    run to five lines, so it steps down to 56 px (× the scale) instead of 64 (§5.3).
  - **Auto-fit:** every role starts at its full size and steps down toward its floor, headline first. If the
    copy still does not fit, a 16:9 or 1.91:1 card drops optional rows (body first, then venue, role or SOURCE,
    then the DOI line) and prints `dropped ...`; the other sizes fail with exit code 2 and a message saying
    what to shorten. Nothing is ever set below a floor. A dropped row still appears in the alt-text sidecar
    but not on the card: put it in the caption as well (always for a stat's SOURCE line), or post the 4:5
    version.
  - **Signatures** follow §5.3: the ATR mark bottom left (12% of the width tall on 1080 px tiles, 8.3% on
    16:9 and 1.91:1 cards) and the Kent State wordmark at the same height with the bottoms aligned, bottom
    right on every size except `youtube-thumb` and staged cards (below), where it moves to the top right of
    the header row. Gold cards carry the navy mark and, at the right, the two-line navy credit "Advanced
    Telerobotics Research Lab / Kent State University" (36 px at 1080) instead of the wordmark; on a staged
    gold card the credit sits top right in three lines, where the wordmark goes on navy. `"signature": "lockup"` swaps the mark for
    horizontal-short (360×137 px on a 1080 px tile, 252×96 px at 1200×675, never under 180 px wide) with the
    wordmark at its height, and prints a warning: use it only for cards viewed at full size, never for feed
    posts (§5.3).
  - **Hazard band:** on the bottom edge of navy cards and the top edge of gold cards, at every size. The 16:9
    PowerPoint files run it down the left edge instead. On `landscape`, the 2:1 crop of an X link card (§4)
    keeps y 37–637 and leaves only a sliver of the band, so make link cards at `linkedin` size or from the
    Landscape PowerPoint file. On `youtube-thumb`, the progress bar covers the band, which is acceptable
    because the band is only decoration.
  - **Stories:** the default `"safe": "reel"` keeps text and logos inside the Meta safe box (x 65–1015,
    y 269–1248; the renders use x 72–1008). `"safe": "story"` keeps only 250 px clear at the top and bottom,
    for a story that is also shared elsewhere. Neither keeps TikTok's right-hand buttons clear (x > 940), so
    make TikTok covers from the Story PowerPoint file.
  - **Art and photos:** `"art"` is `"lattice"` (the default: a seeded constellation in a free corner, skipped
    when there is no room), `"ghost"` (a large faint ATR mark at the right, with the text column narrowed to
    60% of the width; see `announcement-ghost.json`) or `"none"`. `"image"` adds a photo, cover-cropped into a
    band across the top (`"image_position": "top"`, the default for photos on stacked tiles) or a column at
    the right (`"right"`, the default for photos on 16:9 cards). Describe it in `"image_alt"`, and say
    "illustration" there for anything that is not a real photo, as `event-illustrated.json` does. A relative
    image path is looked up in the current directory, then in the spec's folder, then in the skill root.
    `"number"` replaces the icon in the label plate with a series numeral.
  - **The stage (illustrated cards):** a transparent cut-out, such as the concept illustrations in
    `assets/illustrations/`, takes `"image_position": "stage"` by default. It stands bottom right with its
    feet on the signatures' baseline, on a white panel that spans the width above the band (navy) or runs to
    the bottom edge (gold), and at least 45% of its height rises into the field, so it is part of the
    composition rather than a strip above it. The headline runs full width above it; the date, time, place
    and CTA rows sit in the column beside it (about 20 characters per row at 40 px, so write "June 14-18,
    2027", give the time its own row and keep the place short; a place row may take two lines). The navy mark
    stands on the panel bottom left; the Kent State wordmark (navy cards) or the three-line credit (gold
    cards) sits top right. On 16:9 cards the cut-out spans most of the height, the text column runs beside it
    over a pedestal under the cut-out, and the CTA row may drop with a printed warning. The renderer refuses
    to let any text or logo touch the cut-out. `"image_position": "top"` or `"right"` gives a cut-out the
    older mist panel instead.
  - **Alt-text sidecar:** composed from the copy with the key message first (the headline, the number or the
    quote), then who is speaking, then every row and the image description; `"alt_text"` in the spec replaces
    it. Put the key message in the first ~100 characters; the full text equivalent may follow. Because the
    caption repeats the card's details, a concise Instagram alt text is acceptable, and no hard platform
    limit is assumed (X accepts about 1,000 characters). The renderer prints the length and warns when the key
    message first appears after the 100th character.
  - **Examples** in `scripts/examples/social/`: one spec per template, plus `announcement-ghost.json`,
    `event-illustrated.json` (navy, the cut-out on the stage), `outreach-k12.json` (gold, the K-12 card of
    §7.5) and short-copy `*-landscape.json` versions of `event`, `publication` and `recruiting` for the 16:9
    sizes. Most contain `[bracketed]` placeholders on purpose and need `--allow-placeholders`;
    `announcement.json`, `announcement-ghost.json`, `recruiting.json` and `recruiting-landscape.json` render
    as they are. Illustrated cards are for the stacked sizes; on 16:9 they need 3 to 5 words, one date row
    and no CTA.
  - **Flags:** `-o/--out` (default `<spec stem>.png` in the current directory), `--outdir`, `--size`,
    `--sizes square,portrait,story` (the listed sizes, one `<stem>-<size>.png` each), `--all-sizes` (every
    size), `--validate` (check the spec and render nothing), `--allow-placeholders` (drafts only: otherwise
    any `[bracketed]` text is refused with exit code 3), `--safe-zones` (QA overlay of the margins, platform UI,
    badge and art zones), `--no-alt`, `--list-icons`, `--quiet`, `--version`. Exit codes: 0 rendered, 1 spec
    error, 2 layout or contrast error, 3 placeholder refused. `--sizes` and `--all-sizes` carry on after a
    failed size and exit 2 at the end.

  ```
  python scripts/social_card.py scripts/examples/social/announcement.json --size portrait -o announcement.png
  python scripts/social_card.py spec.json --sizes portrait,story --outdir out/
  python scripts/social_card.py spec.json --all-sizes --outdir out/
  ```

  Each render prints the sizes it used, and any warnings after `!` (dropped rows, the lockup, placeholders
  left in the alt text). The first command above prints:

  ```
  ok   announcement.png  1080x1350  title 96px body 44px cta 40px  sig mark 130px KSU 136px bottom-right  alt 283 chars
  ```

  Read that line and look at the card at about 390 px wide before posting. If the script changes,
  `--list-sizes` prints its current floors and signature rules.
- **Backgrounds (the hand-built route):** `assets/illustrations/social-navy-1080x1350.png`,
  `social-gold-1080x1350.png`, `social-navy-1080x1080.png`, `social-gold-1080x1080.png`. Line-work sits at the
  edges and the middle is clear; the measured logo zones are in §5.3. On navy use white or gold text; on gold
  use navy text only (white on gold is 2.0:1 and fails). To build a card, place the background, the logo files
  and live text in any layout tool (PowerPoint, Google Slides, Figma), then export PNG at full size. Use this
  route for a format or layout the templates don't cover.
- Kent State publishes its own Adobe social templates (kent.edu/brand/templates). Those are

### 5.3 Card rules

- **Photos beat graphics.** People and robots doing real work, in natural light, candid rather than posed
  (Kent State's photo guidance: never a subject smiling and looking at the camera at once;
  references/imagery.md). Do not stamp logos on photos: the account already identifies the lab.
- **Type:** Source Sans 3 Bold or Black for headlines, Regular or Semibold for supporting lines; Roboto
  Slab only for a big numeral or a pull quote (references/typography.md). A 1080 px card is shown at about a
  third of its size on a phone, so at 1080 px wide: headline **64 px or larger**, supporting text **40 px or
  larger**, and eyebrows, credits, dates and URLs **36 px or larger**, which is the absolute floor for any text
  on a card (references/typography.md §4.4).
  - **Long titles:** a paper title or a demo-video title, which can run to several lines, is set Bold at
    subhead size instead: **52 px or larger** (typography.md §4.4 puts subheads at 52 to 64 px). The Paper
    accepted and Demo video cover layouts use 52 to 80 px; `social_card.py` takes a paper title down to 56 px
    at 1080 px wide.
  - **Wider canvases:** these pixel floors hold on any canvas from 1080 to 1280 px wide, which covers the
    PowerPoint files and is how brand_check.py tests them. Above 1280 px (1600×900 or a thumbnail exported
    at 3840×2160) scale the floors with the width. `social_card.py` is stricter and scales every size by
    width ÷ 1080 (§5.1).
- **Words:** keep the headline to 12 words or fewer (three to eight read best on a phone). Add at most one
  supporting sentence of about 20 words, four short points or an approved quote of 25 words or fewer, plus the
  date, place and link rows the layout provides. Cut copy until it sits at full size rather than letting it
  shrink. 16:9 cards carry 3 to 5 words (§5.2, type F). Every word on the card also goes in the caption or the
  alt text.
- **Logos on designed cards (types B and C, and D covers):** the ATR signature bottom left and the Kent
  State academic wordmark bottom right, as **two separate signatures** with their own clear space and no
  shared divider (references/logo-system.md §8 and §9).
  - **ATR: the mark, 120 to 130 px tall.** `assets/logos/png/atr-mark-gold-1000.png` on navy,
    `atr-mark-navy-1000.png` on white, mist or gold. logo-system.md §1 allows 90 to 140 px on a 1080 px post;
    use this upper band when the Kent State wordmark sits beside it, so the two match. The templates and
    `social_card.py` both use 130 px on 1080 px tiles. The account name and the caption already carry the
    lab's name. **Why not a horizontal lockup:** a 1080 px card shows at about a third of its size on a
    phone, so at signature size (under 300 px) the full lockup is below its 620 px minimum, and
    horizontal-short's "ADVANCED" falls below the 8 CSS px lettering floor (it would need about 540 px). The
    stacked lockup (`atr-stacked-white-1000.png`, `atr-stacked-navy-1000.png`) is for a featured spot such as
    a carousel's closing slide, at 420 px tall or more for the same reason.
  - **Kent State: the academic wordmark at the same height as the ATR mark** (120 to 130 px tall, which makes
    it 125 to 136 px wide, above the 120 px lab minimum in logo-system.md §8). White on navy, color on white
    or mist, and only on a plain area. Never on gold unless UCM supplies its All Blue file. The files in
    `assets/logos/ksu/` are working copies of the older Stacked raster (colors corrected to #003976 and
    #EFAB00); for a published post, use the official file from kent.edu/brand/logos (logo-system.md §9).
  - **16:9 and 1.91:1 cards** (landscape, link, X and YouTube sizes) are shorter, so both signatures are a
    smaller share of the width: 110 px tall in the 1200 and 1280 px PowerPoint files (wordmark 115 px wide),
    and 8.3% of the width in `social_card.py` (100 px at 1200 wide, 107 at 1280, 133 at 1600; wordmark 105,
    112 and 139 px wide). The wordmark stays at least 100 px wide, its minimum (logo-system.md §7). On
    YouTube thumbnails the PowerPoint file keeps the wordmark bottom right, ending at y 630 above the
    duration badge; `social_card.py` moves it to the top right.
  - **The wordmark's small UNIVERSITY line is accepted as unreadable at feed size.** At 130 px tall that line
    is about 13 px high, about 5 CSS px when a phone shows the card at a third of its size: below the 8 CSS px
    lettering floor that rules out the ATR lockups above. It is accepted for three reasons. The wordmark is
    recognized by its sunburst and the KENT STATE lines (about 14 CSS px on a phone), not by that line. The
    display name, the caption and the alt text all name Kent State University in full, so nothing is lost.
    And a bigger signature would not fix it: at 160 px the line is still under 6 CSS px, and it reaches 8 CSS
    px only with a wordmark more than 210 px tall, which takes the room the headline needs. The floor still
    rules out the ATR lockups because the lab has a lettering-free alternative, the mark; the Kent State
    wordmark is used as supplied. The PowerPoint templates and `social_card.py` follow this rule as written.
  - **Where, on the shipped backgrounds** (line-work measured on the files; clear space is about 43 px around
    a 130 px mark, and every zone below is plain including it):

Headline and text in y 140–1020. (logo-system.md §8 quotes the README's conservative center, y 270–1080; this band just below it is also plain.) |

If a square is needed, use the two clear corners (logo-system.md §8): ATR mark bottom-left (x 60–190, y 890–1020), Kent State wordmark top-right (x 884–1020, y 60–190). |

  - **Gold cards never carry the Kent State wordmark** (until UCM supplies the All Blue file). They carry the

    interface; `social_card.py`'s `story` size clears the Meta box only (§5.1).
- **Color:** navy and white carry most cards; gold is a fill or an accent, never text on white. No gold
  text or thin gold rules on white, no white text on gold (references/color.md).
- **Graphic devices:** on designed cards the hazard-stripe band appears once, as the edge bookend the

- **Charts:** a social chart shows one takeaway with direct labels and the data-viz palette from

## 6. Content pillars and cadence

### 6.1 Pillars

| Pillar | Share | What it covers | Typical formats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Research in motion** | 30% | Robots doing things, experiments, paper and poster explainers (telepresence, tele-embodiment, autonomy, Physical AI) | 15 to 45 s vertical clip; "how it works" carousel; YouTube demo |
| **People of ATR** | 25% | Student and alumni spotlights, a day in the lab, new members, defenses, graduations | Portrait plus quote carousel; short interview clip |
| **Learn robotics** | 20% | Plain-language explainers of robotics terms (references/voice-and-copy.md); how to get into research | Numbered carousel ("7 robotics terms, explained"); 30 s explainer |
| **Outreach and community** | 15% | K-12 workshops, school visits, demo days, Kent State events | Recap carousel; Stories during events |
| **Milestones and news** | 10% | Acceptances, grants, awards, competitions, coverage | Single image or link post |

Promotion (the milestones pillar) stays around 10%; everything else earns attention. Most posts should be
cuts of a **flagship** (a paper, a project milestone, a demo video or an event), not one-off pieces: make
the owned page first, then cut it for each platform (references/pr-events-outreach.md §2).

**Current vs past work.** Market current platforms and projects (for example the Pepper, Unitree Go2 and
Booster K1 work and VendoBot, HRI 2026). The Immersed Pilot Training Simulator (ImmersiFLY) and the
Gesture-Enabled Telepresence Robot are **past** projects: post them only as clearly dated "from the archive"
pieces.

### 6.2 Cadence

A small student team cannot sustain five platforms. Pick **Instagram and LinkedIn plus YouTube**, keep X
for papers and conferences, and use Facebook in camp season if the page is confirmed.

| Channel | Floor (minimum that keeps the account alive) | Target (when a student ambassador owns social) |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram @atr_lab | 1 feed post a week | 2 feed posts a week, plus Stories on event and lab days |
| LinkedIn (canonical page) | 2 posts a month | 1 post a week; the PI and students reshare with their own words |
| YouTube | 1 video per project milestone | 1 video a month, plus Shorts cut from each |
| X @atrlab_kent | Paper threads and conference posts only | The same, plus live posts during conferences |
| Facebook (if confirmed) | K-12 program announcements in registration season | The same |

- **Batch** about 2 hours a week; keep 1 to 2 weeks scheduled; leave room for live event posts.
- **Answer comments daily** (§11); 15 minutes a day covers a lab account.
- **Semester calendar** (typical; verify dates each year): August to September welcome and
  undergraduate recruiting; Oct. 1 TAG Day (#TAGDayKSU); October to December graduate recruiting before Ph.D.
  deadlines, fall conferences, Homecoming (#KentHC), Giving Tuesday (#KSUGives); January to March
  summer-program announcements and registration; March HRI conference (typical); April National Robotics Week and end-of-year showcase; May
  high school internship applications (2026 window: May 11 to 15) and graduations (#FlashesForever for
  alumni); June to July camps; August camp recaps.

## 9. Accessibility

Kent State's required elements and WCAG 2.2 AA (the lab's floor) applied to social:

- **Alt text on every image, in the platform's alt-text field** (not only in the caption). Formula:
  [who or what] + [doing what] + [where or context] + [the detail that makes the post's point]. One or two
  sentences. Don't start with "Image of". Include any text that appears in the image. For charts, give the
  takeaway and put the data on the linked page. Illustrative example (describe the real photo): "A student
  in a VR headset steers a wheeled telepresence robot down a hallway while a second student watches the
  robot's camera feed on a laptop."
- **Captions on every video:** burned-in captions for autoplay plus an uploaded caption file wherever the
  platform accepts one, and a transcript on the owned page (Kent State's required elements ask for captions
  and transcripts). Review auto-captions; never publish them unedited. Narrate what matters visually ("the
  gripper closes on the cup") because audio description is a WCAG AA requirement for prerecorded video.
- **Livestreams** (Instagram Live, YouTube Live, Zoom): turn on live captions, or book real-time captioning
  when the audience is external (WCAG 1.2.4); post the replay with a corrected caption file.
- **No flashing** more than three times per second; keep animations under 5 seconds.
- **CamelCase hashtags** (#HumanRobotInteraction), so screen readers read the words.
- **Emoji:** sparingly and at the end of a sentence (screen readers read each emoji's name). By audience:
  parents 0 to 1; undergraduates 0 to 2 on Instagram; graduate applicants, sponsors, peers and press none.
- **No "fancy" Unicode letters** (bold or script alphabets); screen readers read them as symbols.
- **Plain language**, no long ALL-CAPS sentences and descriptive link text ("Read the paper", never "click
  here"). No link shorteners.
- **Contrast** in graphics follows the brand tokens: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text; never gold text on
  white or white text on gold (references/color.md). Never carry meaning by color alone.
- Full rules and testing: references/accessibility.md.

## 11. Comments, moderation and crisis

**Response times (UCM):** within 4 hours during the workday, within 24 hours during the week, within 48 hours
at weekends. Reply to every genuine comment or question.

| Comment | What to do |
|---|---|
| Question about a program, application or the research | Answer publicly and briefly with the page link. Move personal details to email (a kent.edu address). |
| A minor asking about a program | One public, general answer (§10). No DM. |
| Fair criticism or a factual challenge | Answer once, calmly, with facts or a correction. Do not delete criticism. |
| Fear or hostility about robots or AI | One calm, factual reply at most, or none. Consult the PI or UCM before engaging on anything heated. |
| Spam, scams, threats, harassment, hate, personal information, anything identifying a minor | Hide or delete, and report through the platform. Screenshot first if it may need escalation. |

Delete only content that breaks platform or university rules; never delete criticism to tidy a thread.

**Crisis** (a clip goes viral with a wrong interpretation; a safety incident; a complaint about a minor's
photo; a compromised account):
1. **Pause** scheduled posts on every lab account.
2. **Tell the PI and UCM** at once. UCM speaks for the university; the lab does not issue statements alone.
3. **Remove** any content involving a minor immediately on a guardian's request.
4. **Correct** publicly, once, with facts, when UCM and the PI agree it is needed. No speculation.
5. **Log** what happened and what changed afterwards.

A safety incident in the lab is never social content. The incident steps for events and programs are in
references/pr-events-outreach.md §20.

**Campus emergency or tragedy.** Pause every scheduled post at once, on every lab account. Post nothing
original; share only Kent State's official updates (@KentState, @ksunews), with no hashtags and no lab
promotion. Resume only after UCM signals that normal posting can restart.

**Holding line** (only when UCM approves it; UCM may prefer to post it itself):

> We're aware of [issue] and are working on it with Kent State University. We'll share an update at [link]
> by [time].

**Correction format.** Edit the post where the platform allows it, or reply to it, and pin the correction:
"Correction: an earlier version of this post said [X]. It should have said [Y]." Never delete a wrong post
silently; people have already shared it.

**Compromised account.** From a trusted device: change the password, sign out all other sessions, revoke
connected apps you don't recognize and turn on two-factor authentication. Tell the PI and UCM (social@kent.edu)
at once; UCM's admin access helps recover the account, which is one reason Kent State requires it. Then check
the bio, links and recent posts for anything the intruder changed, and post a short note once the account is
secure.

## 12. Metrics-lite

A 20-minute review **once a month**, not a daily dashboard. Kent State asks for KPIs; these five are enough.

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Saves and shares per post | People found it worth keeping or passing on |
| Video completion rate | The hook and edit worked |
| Link clicks with UTMs (bio link, link posts) | Social is sending people to owned pages |
| Outcomes that month: open-lab visitors, applications, K-12 registrations, sponsor inquiries, email sign-ups | What the lab actually needs |
| Followers | Context only, never a goal |

- **UTMs** on every link: lowercase and hyphens. `utm_source` = where (instagram, linkedin, x, youtube);
  `utm_medium` = social; `utm_campaign` = what and when (summer-workshop-2027, phd-recruiting-2027);
  `utm_content` = variant (bio-link, story, carousel). Keep them in one shared sheet
  (references/web-and-digital.md).
- **Log** each month in a sheet with the columns: month, platform, posts, top 3 posts and why, bottom 3 and why,
  link clicks, outcomes, one change for next month.
- **Each semester:** decide what to stop as well as what to do more of.

## 13. Pre-post checklist

- [ ] One job and one call to action; links go to an owned page with a UTM.
- [ ] Every fact comes from the lab-facts list or a cited source; placeholders are gone; claims keep their hedges.
- [ ] Names and titles follow Kent State style: "Kent State University" then "Kent State", never "KSU" in
      running copy; "College of Sciences and Humanities"; no Oxford comma; "and", not "&", in unit names
      (references/voice-and-copy.md).
- [ ] Handles exactly as in §2; nobody tagged without consent; sponsors only with written approval.
- [ ] Robot footage labeled for speed and teleoperated, autonomous or scripted; simulation labeled; AI images
      labeled "Illustration" and never shown as real.
- [ ] Minors: release on file, no names, no school, no location tag.
- [ ] Alt text in the platform field; captions burned in and uploaded; CamelCase hashtags at the end.
- [ ] Image inside the platform's safe zone; at 1080 px wide, headline 64 px or more, supporting text 40 px or
      more, credits, dates and URLs 36 px or more (nothing smaller); brand contrast pairs only; logos in the
      §5.3 zones.
- [ ] Embargo, anonymity period and invention disclosure cleared for research news.
- [ ] Major items told to UCM a week ahead.

Before a campaign or a new template goes live, also run the social checks in references/qa-checklists.md.
