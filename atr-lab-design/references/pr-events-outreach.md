# PR, events and outreach

How the Advanced Telerobotics Research (ATR) Lab at Kent State University gets its news out through Kent
State's University Communications and Marketing (UCM), announces papers and awards without overclaiming,
runs demo days, lab tours, K-12 programs and conference booths, and handles recruiting, sponsors, alumni,
email and broader-impacts records.

**Read this when** you draft a press release, news submission, media kit, event plan, sign, invitation,
parent or teacher email, recruiting reply, sponsor one-pager or newsletter, or when you log outreach for a
grant report, or when something goes wrong at an event or in the press. Social posts are in
references/social-media.md; voice and the style sheet are in references/voice-and-copy.md; boilerplates, the
proof bank and the verified contact block are in references/brand-foundation.md §9 to §11 (that file wins if a
copy differs); Kent State policies (minors, drones, trademarks) are in references/kent-state-compliance.md.

## 1. Working with Kent State UCM

### 1.1 Who does what

UCM runs media relations for Kent State (kent.edu/ucm/media-relations). The lab does not mass-pitch
reporters on its own. Its job is to **bring UCM good stories early, with the assets and approvals ready**, and
to be a fast, reliable source when UCM or a reporter calls.

| The lab | UCM |
|---|---|
| Spots the story and submits it early | Decides the channel: a pitch to reporters, a news release, **Kent State Today**, **Kent State Magazine**, the homepage or the university's social accounts |
| Supplies the facts, a 150-word plain summary, approved quotes, photos, labeled b-roll and the PI's availability | Writes or edits the release in its own template and distributes it |
| Gets co-author, partner and sponsor approvals | Handles embargoed distribution and reporter contact |
| Answers reporters within hours on release day | Offers **media training** and lists faculty in its **Guide to Experts** |

Two things to set up before the first big story: media training for the PI, and a Guide to Experts listing
for telepresence robotics, teleoperation, human-robot interaction and Physical AI, so UCM can offer the PI
when related news breaks.

### 1.2 How to submit

- **"Submit Your News"** portal: kent.edu/node/276476 (stories, awards, research news).
- **Master communication request form**: kent.edu/node/1009952 (homepage or calendar features, Faculty/Staff
  News Now, university social posts, photo and video shoots).
- **University calendar**: apps.kent.edu/calendar/ (every public event).
- UCM photo and video scheduling: 330-672-8500, photo@kent.edu; 330-672-8502, video@kent.edu
  (kent.edu/brand/images).

### 1.3 Lead times (UCM, verified 2026-09-28)

| Request | Minimum notice |
|---|---|
| Homepage feature or calendar feature | 1 week |
| Faculty/Staff News Now | 1 week |
| A post on Kent State's main social accounts | 7 days |
| UCM photo or video shoot | **3 to 4 weeks** |
| Research news with a journal embargo (lab recommendation) | 4 to 6 weeks before publication `[confirm UCM's preference]` |

Put these into every event and announcement timeline below.

### 1.4 Relationships with journalists

- If a reporter contacts the lab directly: get their name, outlet, deadline and question; tell UCM; call back
  within the deadline.
- Keep a short list of 3 to 5 robotics, technology and regional journalists whose last five stories match the
  lab's topics. Over months: follow their beat, send useful things with no ask and be a fast source when
  they need one. Tell UCM whenever the lab talks to one of them.
- **Op-eds:** a PI op-ed (about 700 words on a timely question the PI can speak to) goes through UCM, which
  checks it against university policy on speaking for the institution and can help place it. Kent State
  faculty publish on The Conversation; ask UCM about membership and process `[VERIFY]`.

## 2. Is it news? Tiers and routing

A lab's story is rarely the robot on its own; it is the people, the problem, the data or the trend. For a lab:

| Story shape | Lab version | Example (placeholders) |
|---|---|---|
| People | A student's path, a first publication, a high school intern who presented research | "[Student] went from summer intern to co-author" (only if true) |
| Small lab, big stage | Competition selections and results | Historical proof: the World Robot Summit 2021 finalist selection (§3.4) |
| The problem | What the research attacks, in human terms | "Delivery robots in public spaces need to show people where they are going" (if the paper says so) |
| Data | A finding with a clear number | "[Finding with number] ([venue], [year])" |
| Milestone | Grant, program launch, new facility, tied to why it matters | "[Grant] will let [N] Northeast Ohio students [do X]" |
| Expert on the news | The PI comments when telepresence, humanoids, drones or VR are in the news | Through UCM's Guide to Experts; never on tragedies |

A local angle (Northeast Ohio students, schools and employers) raises any story's odds.

**Announcement tiers**

| Tier | Examples | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| **Major** | New program, major grant, competition selection or result, new facility | UCM submission (a release if UCM agrees), news post, project page, all social, newsletter, emails to the relevant lists |
| **Medium** | Paper acceptance, conference talk, new project, student award | News post or project update, LinkedIn plus one more platform, newsletter item, tell UCM |
| **Minor** | New member, new equipment, small updates | People page update, one Instagram post or Story |

**Create once, distribute twice.** Build the owned page first (project or publication page, news post), then
cut it for every channel: the social recipes in references/social-media.md §7, a newsletter item, a slide and
a QR code for talks (references/presentations.md). Write the four lengths first: a 7 to 10-word tag, a
one-sentence summary of 25 words or fewer, a 50-word blurb and a 150-word plain summary
(references/voice-and-copy.md). Every other piece reuses them.

## 3. Research, award and grant announcements

### 3.1 Research-news timeline

| When | Step | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance | Log the paper; decide if it is release-worthy (§2); check embargo and anonymity rules (§3.2); check whether an invention disclosure is needed | PI |
| T−6 to −4 weeks | Notify UCM with the publication or embargo date, the 150-word plain summary and the PI's availability | PI or lab communications lead |
| T−4 weeks | Request a UCM photo or video shoot if wanted; gather lab b-roll with speed and autonomy labels | Lab |
| T−3 weeks | Draft the release pieces (§4); co-author and partner-institution approvals; funder acknowledgment check | UCM and lab |
| T−2 weeks | PI approves the final text; partners approve; assets finalized with alt text and credits | PI |
| T−1 week | UCM pitches under embargo; the lab prepares social posts, the newsletter item, the news post and the project-page update | UCM and lab |
| T0 (embargo lifts) | Publish the news post and page updates; social; newsletter; UCM distribution | Everyone |
| T+1 to +7 days | Answer reporters within hours; share coverage; log coverage and inquiries (§5.3) | Lab |

### 3.2 Embargoes, preprints and disclosure

- **Journal embargoes** are set by the publisher. Press material goes only to journalists who agree to honor
  it; state the exact date, time and time zone ("[Weekday, Mon. D, YYYY], [9 a.m. / 10:30 a.m.] ET").
  Nothing appears on the lab website or social before it lifts. UCM handles embargoed distribution.
- **Conference papers:** publicize only after acceptance is public (the accepted-papers list or camera-ready),
  in the form the venue allows.
- **Double-blind venues** may restrict publicizing work under review, including talks and videos that reveal
  authorship. Check the venue's current policy `[VERIFY per venue]`.
- **Preprints** (arXiv) are public on posting; an embargo cannot be applied afterwards.
- **Posting PDFs:** check the publisher's author-posting rules (IEEE, ACM and Springer differ) before hosting
  a paper on the lab site.
- **Inventions:** a talk, poster, video, release or post can affect patent rights. If the work may be
  patentable, file the invention disclosure with Kent State's Office of Technology Commercialization **before**
  any public reveal.
- **Sponsor agreements** may include publicity or prepublication-review clauses. Check through the Office of
  Sponsored Programs before naming a sponsor or releasing results.
- **Funders:** acknowledge the award as its terms require; for NSF, see §18.2. Never imply a funder endorses
  the lab, a product or a claim.

### 3.3 Award, grant and competition fact checklist

Labs overclaim here most often: a finalist becomes a winner, a nomination becomes an award, a team award
becomes one student's, a grant is announced before it is official. Fill every line before drafting, and
store the result with the lab's verified facts (the proof bank) so every later mention reuses the same wording.

- [ ] Exact name of the award, grant or result, spelled as the conferring body writes it
- [ ] Conferring body (conference, organizer, agency, foundation)
- [ ] Year, and the date the body announced it
- [ ] Recipients exactly (the team, a named student, a paper's authors), each named with consent
- [ ] What was recognized (paper title, project, entry)
- [ ] Level: winner, award, finalist, honorable mention, nominee, selected to compete, accepted
- [ ] Source URL from the conferring body, or the official program, record or letter. A lab post alone is
      not enough for a new claim.
- [ ] Money: only if the body published the amount and the PI approves printing it
- [ ] Grants: the lab's role (PI, co-PI, key personnel, subaward) and the award number from the official record

**Timing.** Announce only after the conferring body has announced publicly or given written permission.
Announce a grant only after it is official (the Office of Sponsored Programs has the executed award, or it
appears in the agency's public record, for NSF nsf.gov/awardsearch) and the PI approves `[VERIFY the OSP
step]`. Never announce "pending", "recommended for funding" or "under negotiation".

### 3.4 Never upgrade

| The source says | Write | Never write |
|---|---|---|
| Nominee, shortlisted | "nominated for", "shortlisted for" | "won", "award-winning" |
| Finalist, one of N finalists | "finalist", "one of N finalists" | "winner", "placed", "award" |
| Honorable mention | "honorable mention" | "award" on its own |
| Selected to compete | "selected to compete in" | "competed and won", "placed" |
| Selected for an onsite event | "selected for the onsite challenge" | "competed at [site]" unless the lab confirms the team was there |
| Team award | "the [team name] team received" | one student "received" it alone |
| Award to one member | "[Student], a member of the lab, received" | "the ATR Lab won" |
| Co-PI on a multi-institution grant | "[PI] is a co-principal investigator on [project], funded by [agency]" | "the lab's $[total] grant", "led" |
| The director's pre-Kent State grants or media coverage | leave out, or date them and attribute them to the director | "ATR Lab funding", "featured in 500+ outlets" |
| A workshop or special-session award | its exact title, e.g. "Special Session Best Award" | "Best Paper at [main conference]" |

**Verified worked examples** (checked 2026-09-28; confirm exact award names against the conferring body
before reuse):
- **World Robot Summit 2020/2021 (postponed):** the team was "selected as one of 11 finalists" in the Plant
  Disaster Prevention Challenge. The Kent Stater (March 17, 2021) reported it was the only team from the United
  States: attribute that to The Kent Stater unless WRS confirms it. Source:
  kentstater.com/2188/uncategorized/kent-state-advanced-telerobotic-research-team-to-compete-in-the-world-robot-summit/
- **NASA SUITS 2020:** "selected as one of the top 10 teams to participate in onsite NASA SUITS challenge at
  Johnson Space Center". Write "selected as a top-10 team for the onsite challenge"; do not write that the team
  competed at Johnson Space Center unless the lab confirms it traveled there. Source:
  atr.cs.kent.edu/atr-accepted-for-nasa-suits-challenge/
- **WRS Distinguished Paper Award, 2021:** listed on the director's page (cs.kent.edu/~jkim/); confirm the exact name, paper and
  recipients with the PI before use `[VERIFY]`.
- **RoboCup:** no placements are published for any year. **Never claim one.**

## 4. Press release template

UCM may write the release in its own template; the lab then supplies these pieces. Either way the structure
is AP inverted pyramid: the most important facts first, background later.

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
[Release date: Mon. D, YYYY]
(or: EMBARGOED UNTIL [Weekday, Mon. D, YYYY], [9 a.m. / 10:30 a.m.] ET)

[Headline in sentence case: active verb, what is new, about 12 words or fewer, no jargon]
[Subhead: one sentence that adds the "so what" or the key number]

KENT, Ohio — [Lede, 35 words or fewer: who did what, and why it matters to people.]

[Nut graf: the context and the problem, 2 to 3 sentences.]

"[Quote that interprets or adds meaning, not one that repeats facts]," said Jong-Hoon Kim,
associate professor of computer science and director of the Advanced Telerobotics Research Lab.

[How it works, in plain language, 1 to 2 paragraphs. Keep the paper's hedges: sample size,
lab setting, simulation vs hardware, preliminary.]

"[Second quote: a student researcher, partner or teacher. Real and approved in writing]," said
[Name], [title or year and major].

[What's next and the limitations, 1 paragraph.]

[Funding sentence exactly as the award terms require. For NSF, the acknowledgment and disclaimer
in §18.2, copied verbatim, never style-edited.]

The study, "[Title]," [appears in / will be presented at] [venue, date]. DOI: [DOI].

About the Advanced Telerobotics Research Lab
[100-word boilerplate from references/brand-foundation.md §10, verbatim]

About Kent State University
[Kent State boilerplate, supplied by UCM]

Media contact: [UCM media relations contact, as UCM specifies]
Lab contact (scheduling only): [name], [kent.edu email]

###

Multimedia (each with a credit line, alt text and usage terms):
- [photo-filename.jpg]: [caption]. Credit: [photographer / Kent State University]
- [b-roll link]: [what it shows; playback speed; teleoperated / autonomous]
```

**Rules**
- **Dateline** "KENT, Ohio —". Use "OH" only in a full postal address with a ZIP code.
- **Headline formulas:** "[System] lets [people] [do something] [where or under what conditions]"; "Kent State
  researchers [found / built] [thing], which could [careful outcome]"; "[Recipient] receives [exact award
  name] from [body]"; "[Team] named [one of N finalists] in [competition, year]".
- **Quotes** are real and approved in writing by the person quoted. Never invent or "polish" a quote beyond
  what the person approves.
- **Re-check the director's title** in the Kent State catalog before each release (verified as associate
  professor of computer science on 2026-09-28).
- **Names:** full name and lowercase title on first reference; after that, last name only ("Kim said"). No
  "Dr." in news copy (references/voice-and-copy.md §3.1). Students: "[Name], a senior computer science
  major" or "a first-year student", never "freshman".
- **Headlines** are sentence case, never all caps (references/voice-and-copy.md §3.4). UCM may apply its own
  house style when it edits the release `[VERIFY with UCM]`.
- **Dates and times** in AP form: "Tuesday, Sept. 29, 2026", "9 a.m. ET", never "09:00 a.m." or "September
  29th".
- No "revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "first" or "only" without a source.
- **Required legal text is never style-edited.** Funder acknowledgments, disclaimers and nondiscrimination
  lines are copied exactly as the source gives them, even where they look odd in house style (the NSF lines'
  "award No." and "author(s)" stay as written; §18.2).
- **Lab boilerplate:** use the 100-word version in references/brand-foundation.md §10, unchanged; it is the
  canonical copy, so do not keep another version here. To name the college, use that section's "College of
  Sciences and Humanities" variation, and end with "Learn more at www.atr.cs.kent.edu." if the release has no
  other link.
- **Kent State boilerplate:** request the current version from UCM; never write one. It usually carries UCM's
  approved R1 sentence (kent.edu/ucm/c-d, "Carnegie Classification"); use that wording verbatim and re-check
  it before each release, because Carnegie revised its classifications in 2025.

**Pitch quality bar** (anything the lab hands UCM or sends a reporter)
- [ ] A clear news hook: something that just happened or is about to
- [ ] A reporter could write the story from the material alone: finding, quote, person, visuals, contact
- [ ] Subject line under 50 characters that predicts the headline; pitch under 150 words; a link to the media
      kit instead of attachments
- [ ] The ask is explicit (interview, embargoed copy, lab visit, footage)
- [ ] The PI can respond within hours on release day
- [ ] Follow up once on day 3 with new information and once on day 7 with a fresh angle, then stop

Before release, score the draft from four points of view, 1 to 10 each: a UCM media officer (newsworthiness,
AP style), a skeptical science journalist (hype, missing evidence), the PI (accuracy) and a local reader
(clarity, "so what"). Revise until each scores 7 or more and the average is 8 or more. General QA lists are in
references/qa-checklists.md.

## 6. Interviews and expert requests

- Prepare **three key messages** and one plain analogy; practice bridging back to them.
- Say the important things in a form that can be quoted and transcribed: "The Advanced Telerobotics Research
  Lab at Kent State University studies [...]", with numbers spoken aloud.
- No speculation beyond the evidence; no claims about safety, autonomy or products the paper does not support.
- **NSF-funded work:** acknowledge NSF support aloud in every media interview, including podcasts, radio,
  television and social media video. Current NSF award terms require it (Grant General Conditions, Art. 28.a.4;
  PAPPG 24-1 ch. XI.E.4.a for older awards); see §18.2.
- Before agreeing to a recorded talk or podcast, confirm the recording and the right to republish it. If an
  event does not record, record a re-delivery within a week.
- On camera in the lab: clear whiteboards and screens of unpublished results, confidential sponsor material
  and anything awaiting an invention disclosure; label robot footage honestly.

## 7. Events: rules for every event

- **20% event, 80% before and after.** Events fail on invitations and follow-up, not on the day.
- **One primary outcome** per event, measured: applications, registrations, sponsor meetings or email
  sign-ups. Headcount alone is a vanity number.
- **Recurring beats one-off.** An annual demo day and a yearly camp compound lists, relationships and content.
- **Capture context, not only contacts.** After each real conversation, note what the person cares about and
  the agreed next step. Follow up within 24 to 48 hours: hot contacts get a personal note, warm contacts one
  relevant resource, scan-only contacts one light touch or nothing.
- **Every event is a content engine:** photograph with consent, cut clips, write the recap post and the
  newsletter item (references/social-media.md §7.9).
- **Listing checklist** (Kent State calendar, the lab site, social): title (what, and for whom); date, time and
  time zone; room and building; parking; cost; registration link; an accessibility statement with a contact for
  accommodations; the photo policy; one image with alt text; Event or EducationEvent schema on the lab page
  (references/web-and-digital.md).
- **Robot safety at any public event:** marked demo zones; an operator at each robot with the emergency stop
  within reach; low speeds for mobile robots in crowds; no visitor operates hardware without a briefing;
  drones only inside netted areas, or not at all. **Any outdoor flight on campus** needs approval from Kent
  State's UAS review committee, with the pre-flight request filed at least two weeks ahead, and a Part 107
  remote pilot certificate holder present (policy 5-12.16, "Kent state university is a 'no drone fly zone'").
- **Print** through Kent State's contracted printers and promotional vendors (kent.edu/brand/vendors).
  Giveaways carrying the ATR or Kent State marks need UCM approval and a licensed vendor
  (references/print-and-merch.md).
- **Minors:** if minors attend as an organized group program, check with Compliance and Risk Management whether
  it is a covered program under the minors policy (open public events are generally excluded by the policy's
  definition) `[VERIFY with Compliance]`. Ask **at least 12 weeks out**: a covered program must be registered
  no later than 60 days before minors first take part, with every authorized adult named (§8.1, §10.1).
- **When something goes wrong** (an injury, a robot incident, a complaint, a reporter at the door): §20.

## 9. Lab tours

Tours are small, frequent and easy to run badly. Keep them short, safe and specific.

### 9.1 Types

| Tour | Typical guests | Length | Primary outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prospective students | Graduate applicants, undergraduates, admitted students | 30 min | Application or open-lab return |
| School group | K-12 class with teachers; **book at least [10] weeks ahead** (below) | 45 min | A request for a program or a return visit |
| Partner visit | Industry or sponsor contacts (cleared first, §15.1) | 45 to 60 min | A concrete next step with a date |
| Leadership, press, VIP | Deans, visitors, reporters (with UCM) | 20 to 30 min | A story they can retell |

### 9.2 Before the tour

- [ ] Guest list, and whether minors are coming (teachers stay with their students throughout). A lab-run
      visit for a school group is likely a covered program under Policy 3342-5-19, which must be registered at
      least 60 days before the visit, so tell schools: "Book at least [10] weeks ahead so we can complete Kent
      State's registration for programs with minors." `[VERIFY with Compliance]`
- [ ] 3 to 4 stops chosen for this audience, each with a named student host
- [ ] Benches, whiteboards and screens cleared of unpublished results, confidential sponsor material and
      anything awaiting an invention disclosure
- [ ] Robots charged; zones marked; one person owns safety
- [ ] Photo permission: releases for non-affiliated visitors and minors, or no photos of them
- [ ] A leave-behind for this audience (a one-pager, the program flyer or the recruiting card)

### 9.3 Script skeleton

1. **At the door (2 minutes):** who we are in one sentence (the lab's verified self-description, in plain
   words), the safety rules ("Stay behind the tape unless a host invites you in. Ask before touching any
   robot."), and what they will see.
2. **Stop 1, a robot in motion:** start with something moving; attention is highest at the start.
3. **Stop 2, a student and their project:** the **30-second explanation**: "I work on [project]. It lets
   [who] [do what] by [how]. Right now we're trying to [current question]. Want to try [thing]?"
4. **Stop 3, where the work goes:** papers, competitions (in their exact wording, §3.4) and outreach.
5. **Close (2 minutes):** one call to action for this audience ("Come to open lab on [day]", "Request a
   program for your class", "Let's set up [next step] on [date]"), and the leave-behind.

Follow up within 24 to 48 hours from the notes.

### 18.2 NSF acknowledgment and disclaimer

Every publication based on NSF-funded work, including web pages, flyers, posters, slides, newsletters and
releases, carries the acknowledgment. All of them except articles in scientific, technical or professional
journals also carry the disclaimer. Use the wording in the terms the award letter cites, copied verbatim and
never style-edited. The house copy of these lines is in references/voice-and-copy.md §3.6.

**Current NSF terms** (every Grant General Conditions edition since Oct. 1, 2024, now the July 31, 2026 edition,
Art. 28; also awards made on or after May 20, 2024 under the Research Terms and Conditions):

> This material is based upon work supported by the U.S. National Science Foundation under award No. [NSF
> award number].
>
> Any opinions, findings and conclusions or recommendations expressed in this material are those of the
> author(s) and do not necessarily reflect the views of the U.S. National Science Foundation.

**Older awards** (made under earlier terms, such as those of Jan. 30, 2023) follow the terms their award letter
cites. PAPPG 24-1 (ch. XI.E.4) gives the same two sentences with "the National Science Foundation" (no "U.S.")
and "Award No. [NSF award number]", and those terms have no logo clause.

In running copy write "U.S. National Science Foundation" on first mention, then "NSF" (references/voice-and-copy.md
§3.6). Art. 28 and NSF's brand standards also require:
- **The NSF full-color logo** with the written acknowledgment on products of NSF-funded work under the current
  terms: web pages, educational, press, exhibit and event materials, reports, infographics, social media and
  video. Use only the files from NSF's brand portal, unaltered, at least 0.625 in, and furthest left in any row
  of funder logos. Never put it on business cards, email signatures, professional profiles or a social profile
  picture. Ask NSF for brand clearance (NSFbranding@nsf.gov) before publishing a web page or producing exhibit,
  video or promotional items. Logo placement is set in the format files (references/posters.md,
  references/presentations.md, references/quad-charts.md).
- **Oral acknowledgment** of NSF support in every media interview, including podcasts, radio, television and
  social media video (§6).
- **Copies for NSF:** give the NSF program officer access to a copy of each publication based on the award
  (a release, web page, flyer or newsletter), labeled with the award number, promptly after it is published.

Sources: nsf.gov/awards/terms-conditions (GC-1, July 31, 2026, Art. 28);
nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-11-other-post-award-requirements; nsf.gov/policies/brand (brand policy, standards
manual and fact sheet for award recipients). Re-check them before each release.

Other agencies (NASA, DoD, state programs) have their own wording in the award terms; copy it from there. Quad
charts and sponsor slides have their own rules: references/quad-charts.md.

## 20. Issues, incidents and negative press

Rare, but the lab runs robots in front of the public and hosts minors, so plan for it. The first job is always
people, then facts, then words. Social steps (pausing posts, holding lines, corrections, compromised accounts)
are in references/social-media.md §11.

**At an event or program** (an injury, a robot fault or collision, a lost child, a safety complaint):
1. **Stop the demo or activity** and care for the people involved. Call 911 for any emergency. Hit the robot's
   emergency stop and keep people back.
2. **Follow Kent State's incident-reporting procedure** for the event or program (the program's written
   emergency procedure, Public Safety, Compliance and Risk Management) `[VERIFY the reporting route with
   Compliance and Risk Management and Public Safety]`.
3. **K-12 programs:** phone the parent or guardian first, then email (§13.5).
4. **Tell the PI and UCM at once.** UCM speaks to the media for Kent State; lab members don't comment on an
   incident, speculate about causes or post about it.
5. **Keep a log:** what happened, when, who was there, what was done, who was told. Keep photos and robot
   logs as evidence; don't post them.
6. **Pause related promotion** (event posts, recap plans) until UCM and the PI agree what to say.

**Negative press or a hostile inquiry.** Take the reporter's name, outlet, deadline and question, and pass them
to UCM within the hour (§1.4). Don't answer on the spot, don't go "off the record" and don't argue on social.
If a story gets facts wrong, send UCM the specific error and the source that shows it; UCM asks the outlet for
the correction.

**Afterwards:** a short debrief with the PI (what changed: the safety plan, staffing, signage, consent
wording) and an update to the relevant checklist in this file.
