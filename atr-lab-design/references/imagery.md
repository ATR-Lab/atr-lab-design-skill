# Imagery: photography, video and illustration

How the ATR Lab shows itself in pictures: what to photograph and film, how to light, frame and treat it, how to put
text over photos, the consent, safety, confidentiality and licensing rules, and when to use the lab's three
concept illustrations instead. Real photos of real work are the lab's strongest proof; everything here protects
their quality and their honesty.

**Read this when** you plan or direct a shoot or a demo video, pick or edit photos for any piece, put text on a
photo, write alt text or photo credits, use photos of K-12 participants, use someone else's robot images, or
consider an illustration or AI image instead of a photo.

## 1. Principles and the image hierarchy

Kent State's photo direction fits a working lab well: "use available natural light when possible, demonstrate
purpose and engagement and avoid staging photos for authentic and honest storytelling"
(https://www.kent.edu/brand/images). Candid, editorial, action over posing. KSU's rule of thumb: a subject may
smile or look at the camera, not both (https://www.kent.edu/ucm/web-team/images-graphics-and-video). Headshots are
the exception.

Pick images in this order:

| Rank | Image type | Use it for | Never for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Real photo or video of the lab's own work** | Anything about who the lab is and what it did: web, social, press, posters, quad-chart evidence | (nothing; this is the default) |
| 2 | **Real figure or screenshot** (plot, RViz or Gazebo view, UI), labelled "simulation" where it is | Results, methods, technical slides | Implying hardware results from simulation |
| 3 | **Diagram built from the brand geometry and icons** | Architecture, process, concepts | Pretending to be a photo |
| 4 | **Concept illustration** (`assets/illustrations/illus-*.png`) | Explaining an idea when no real image exists or can be used | Evidence, results, "our lab" |
| 5 | **Abstract background** (`assets/illustrations/bg-*`, `social-*`, patterns) | Covers, dividers, text posts | Standing in for content |

The lab's current website mixes cluttered wide-angle phone shots with a stock cockpit photo standing in for the
pilot-training simulator. Both are what this file fixes: tidy, well-lit photos of the real thing, and never stock
or AI imagery presented as the lab's work.

## 2. Shot list

Shoot every item in landscape and portrait (social needs both), and wide, medium and close.

| Subject | What to capture | Framing tips | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Robots in action** | The robot doing its task (grasping, walking, navigating, talking to a person), not parked | Eye level with the robot (get low for small robots); a clear subject against a calm background; shoot the complete action so you can pick the peak frame | Web heroes, social, posters, press |
| **Hands on hardware** | Wiring, tuning a gripper, mounting a sensor, soldering (with PPE) | Tight crop on hands and the part; shallow depth of field; faces optional | Recruiting, program pages, social carousels |
| **Teleoperation rigs** | Operator and robot in one frame, or a matched pair: operator at the controls, then the robot responding | Show the link: over-the-shoulder of the operator with the robot or its video feed visible | Research pages, talks, press |
| **VR sessions** | Person in the headset plus what they see (the headset view on a monitor behind or beside them) | Put the monitor in frame and expose for it; a second shot of the screen alone | Telepresence and immersive-teleoperation stories |
| **K-12 builds** | Students building and testing robots, teamwork, the final showcase | Hands and projects first; faces only with a signed release (section 5); no name tags or school logos in close-ups | Outreach flyers, program pages, family emails |
| **Demo days and open lab** | Visitors trying a demo with a student host, the crowd at a station, the welcome sign | Wide establishing shot, then candid interactions. Photo-notice signage is notice, not a release: identifiable visitors need releases (section 5), otherwise shoot wide or from behind | Recaps, next year's invitations, sponsor reports |
| **Competitions and field trials** | Team at the venue, robot on the course, the team with its robot | Documentary; include venue context | News posts (verified results only; no RoboCup placements exist to claim) |
| **Environmental portraits** | Members at their bench or with their robot | Subject on a third, robot or lab softly behind; natural expression | People pages, speaker bios, press kits |
| **Headshots** | Head and shoulders, looking at the camera | Eye level, even soft light, plain mist or softly blurred lab background; export 200 × 300 px for kent.edu profiles | Directory, bios |
| **Details** | Grippers, sensors, joints, the mark on a robot or door sign, a checked screen | Macro or tight crop, clean surface | Texture shots for carousels, section openers |

## 3. Lighting, framing and backgrounds

**Lighting**

- Natural window light first; turn off mixed light sources you can control, or set white balance to the dominant
  one. Lab fluorescents plus daylight give green-magenta casts that are hard to fix.
- Bounce a flash or LED off the ceiling or a white card; never a direct on-camera flash (flat faces, hard shadows,
  glare on screens and visors).
- Screens and headsets: lower the screen brightness, expose for the screen and angle the camera to avoid
  reflections.
- Avoid strobing robot LEDs and indicator lights in video (see section 10).

**Framing**

- Default landscape; leave calm space where text will go. For slide use, keep the left half calmer (the title
  backgrounds keep the left 55 % clear, see references/graphic-elements.md).
- Rule of thirds, subject looking or moving into the frame. A 45° viewing angle on a robot often reads best and
  echoes the brand geometry.
- Straighten verticals and horizons; crop out distractions rather than cloning them.
- Leave margins for platform crops: Instagram shows 4:5 posts as a 3:4 grid tile; stories lose the top 14 % and the
  bottom 35 % to UI (references/social-media.md).

**Backgrounds**

- Tidy the bench before shooting: cables, trash, drinks, bags, personal items, sticky notes and packaging out of
  frame.
- A plain wall, a mist or navy backdrop, or the lab softly out of focus beats a cluttered bench.
- Check the background for screens, whiteboards, badges and door codes (section 7) and for safety issues
  (section 6).

## 4. Colour treatment and text over photos

**Default treatment: true colour.** Correct white balance and exposure, crop and straighten, remove dust.
Natural skin tones; never brand-tint people.

**Not allowed:** Instagram-style filters, heavy HDR, fake bokeh, vignettes, AI "enhance", generative fill or
expand, and any edit that changes what happened: removing a tether, cable, safety net, support stand or the
operator (that misrepresents autonomy), adding objects, or compositing separate shots into one "moment". Blur is a
last resort for an incidental background face; never blur and publish a minor who is the subject; pick another
shot.

**Allowed treatments** (only for photos used as backgrounds behind text):

| Treatment | Recipe | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Navy scrim** | Flat navy `#003976` layer over the photo (or midnight `#00295F`) | Any text over a photo | Other scrim colours (black, gold, gradients in other hues) |
| **Graded scrim** | Navy from 85 % opacity at the text side fading to 0 % across the photo | Hero banners, title slides with a photo | Text in the faded part |
| **Navy duotone** | Grayscale mapped from midnight `#00295F` (shadows) to mist `#F3F6FA` (highlights), then a navy scrim under the text | Section openers and covers where the photo is atmosphere | Portraits, evidence photos, people as the main subject |

No gold duotones or gold tints: gold is an accent colour, and gold over photos rarely reaches contrast.

**Contrast over photos.** Measured worst case (a pure-white photo pixel under the scrim):

| Scrim over the photo | White text | Gold text |
|---|---|---|
| navy 60 % | 3.7:1, large text only | 1.8:1, never |
| navy 70 % | 4.9:1, body text OK | 2.4:1, never |
| navy 80 % | 6.5:1 | 3.2:1, large display text only |
| navy 90 % | 8.6:1 | 4.3:1, large text only |
| midnight 80 % | 7.8:1 | 3.9:1, large text only |

So: **white body text needs at least a 70 % navy scrim** (use 75-80 % for projection); large white headlines need 60 %;
gold text over photos only as large display type on an 80 %+ scrim. If the photo area is uniformly dark you may
measure the real local contrast and use less. Kent State's own hero-video spec uses a 50 % Kent State blue overlay,
which is fine for mood but not enough for text over bright footage.

Recipes:

- pptxgenjs: `slide.addShape(pptx.shapes.RECTANGLE, { x, y, w, h, fill: { color: "003976", transparency: 25 } })`
  (transparency 25 = 75 % opaque), placed between the photo and the text.
- CSS: `background: linear-gradient(90deg, rgba(0,57,118,.85) 0 45%, rgba(0,57,118,0) 80%), url(photo.jpg) center/cover;`
  with the text inside the first 45 %.
- Pillow duotone:
  ```python
  from PIL import Image, ImageOps
  g = ImageOps.autocontrast(Image.open("photo.jpg").convert("L"), cutoff=1)
  ImageOps.colorize(g, black="#00295F", white="#F3F6FA").save("photo-duotone.jpg", quality=85)
  ```
- Logos on photos: only on a calm area, white or two-tone reverse on dark, navy on light; otherwise add a scrim
  (references/logo-system.md). Never put patterns over photos.

## 5. People and consent

Who needs what before an image is used (KSU rules from https://www.kent.edu/ucm/photography-and-videography and
https://www.kent.edu/ucm/web-team/images-graphics-and-video; lab practice marked):

| Person | Before publishing | Also |
|---|---|---|
| Kent State students, faculty, staff | Ohio law lets the university use their likeness for educational and promotional purposes (ORC 2741.09(A)(5)); **written permission to name a student** in a photo | Respect FERPA directory opt-outs. KSU gives special consideration to international students who are culturally averse to photography: ask first. Lab practice: tell people when you are shooting and honour every "please don't" |
| Adults not affiliated with Kent State (visitors, sponsors, parents, collaborators from other institutions) | A signed **model release**. KSU: "Individuals who are not employees, faculty or students of the university must sign a model release form" (https://www.kent.edu/ucm/photography-and-videography) `[UCM model release form link]` | Keep the signed form with the photo log. Event signage does not replace it (see below) |
| **Minors** (K-12 workshops, internships, visits) | **KSU text:** UCM says "Special consideration and limitations apply to minors shown in photography" (https://www.kent.edu/ucm/photography-and-videography), and Policy 5-19 requires parents or guardians to "execute all relevant forms and releases as may be required by the particular program" (https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-campus-activities-involving-minors). **Lab practice (stricter than the KSU text):** a signed parent or guardian photo/video release for that program, on file before any image is used | See the minors rules below; confirm the current forms with Compliance and Risk Management `[VERIFY]` |

**Signage is notice, not a release.** A "photography in progress" sign or a "no photos" lanyard tells people what is
happening; it does not meet the KSU release rule. Any identifiable non-affiliated adult in a published photo still
needs a signed model release, and any minor a guardian release. Without them, use wide or back-of-head shots in
which no one is identifiable.

**Minors: extra care (lab practice on top of the policy)**

- No faces of minors without a release on file. If you are not sure, the image is not used. Without releases,
  shoot hands, projects, backs of heads or wide shots in which no one is identifiable.
- Never name a minor in a caption, post, alt text or file name; never pair a face with a school name. Keep name
  tags, school logos and uniforms out of close-ups.
- Turn off location tags; strip GPS metadata from files before sharing: turn off location in the export dialog, or
  run `exiftool -gps:all= -overwrite_original photo.jpg` (without `-overwrite_original`, exiftool leaves a
  `photo.jpg_original` backup that still holds the location). Confirm with `exiftool -gps:all photo.jpg`, which
  should print nothing. (Install with `brew install exiftool`.)
- At events: photo-notice signage at the entrance and a coloured lanyard or sticker for "no photos"; brief every
  photographer on who is marked. The signage is notice only: the releases above still apply to every identifiable
  person.
- Share camp photos with families only after checking the release list; post nothing to social until the list
  is checked for every face.
- The concept illustration `assets/illustrations/illus-k12-robot-build.png` is the fallback for outreach pieces
  when no consented photos exist.

**Photo log** (a spreadsheet per shoot): file name, date, event or project, people (adults by name; minors by
"release on file: yes/no" only), release location, photographer and credit, restrictions (internal only, sponsor
approval needed, embargo), and where it has been published. If someone asks for a photo to come down, remove it
promptly and note it in the log.

**Kent State photographers:** UCM photo scheduling (photo@kent.edu, 330-672-8500) and video (video@kent.edu,
330-672-8502) need 3-4 weeks' notice. Their archive (https://kentstate.photoshelter.com) is university property:
use it only with permission.

## 6. Safety in photos

A photo is a public record of how the lab works. Publish only safe practice.

- **PPE matches the task:** safety glasses for soldering, cutting, drilling and battery work; closed-toe shoes;
  long hair tied back and no loose lanyards or sleeves near moving parts; gloves where the task needs them. Follow
  `[Lab safety SOP]`.
- **Powered robots:** the operator's emergency stop is within reach and, in wide shots, visible; no one inside a
  moving arm's workspace; demo zones marked; mobile robots at walking speed around people.
- **Drones:** indoor flights inside nets. Kent State is a "no drone fly zone": any outdoor flight, including for
  promotional B-roll, needs UAS review committee approval (pre-flight request at least two weeks ahead) and a Part
  107 remote pilot certificate holder (policy 5-12.16,
  https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-unmanned-aircraft-systems). Ask the committee
  whether indoor lab flights are covered `[VERIFY]`.
- **Batteries and tools:** no charging LiPo packs, open power supplies or tools left live in the frame.
- Never stage risk for drama (hands in a gripper, someone under a drone). If a shot shows an unsafe practice, do not
  publish it; reshoot.

## 7. Confidentiality and export control

Check every frame, background included, before it leaves the lab:

- **Screens:** close email, chat, grade books, rosters and anything with student data (FERPA); passwords, API keys,
  tokens and Wi-Fi details; unpublished results; sponsor data. Use a clean desktop or a dedicated demo screen.
- **Whiteboards and notebooks:** erase or shoot them out of frame. Unpublished ideas can matter for patents: any
  public reveal of a new invention (photo, video, poster, demo) waits until the PI confirms an invention disclosure
  where one is needed (Kent State's Office of Technology Commercialization handles them in confidence).
- **Embargoes:** results in a paper under embargo stay off every image until the embargo lifts.
- **Sponsor and partner hardware:** prototypes, NDA hardware and partner logos need the partner's written approval.
- **Export control:** hardware, software and technical data on some sponsored projects (typically defense or space)
  can be export-controlled, and detailed photos or video of them can count as technical data. If a project carries
  a sponsor restriction, an ITAR or EAR marking, or a technology control plan, don't photograph it for marketing
  without PI sign-off and a check with Kent State's export control office `[export control office contact, verify]`.
  Public sponsor documents such as NASA SBIR briefing charts must contain no ITAR or proprietary content.
- **Access details:** ID badges, keycards, door codes and network labels stay out of frame.

## 8. Third-party, stock and archive images

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Press or marketing images of commercial robots (Boston Dynamics Atlas or Spot, and the like) | Copyrighted. Don't use them without a licence or written permission and a credit. The lab's old quad-chart template used Atlas and Spot press images: replace them with the lab's own images or a diagram |
| The lab's own commercial robots (for example its Pepper, Unitree Go2 and Booster K1) | Photograph them freely. Manufacturer logos may appear incidentally; don't imply endorsement or partnership, and don't make a manufacturer's logo the hero of an ad or merch |
| Stock photos | Only with a valid licence, only for generic context (a campus skyline, a city), and never as a stand-in for the lab's own robots, people or projects |
| Figures or photos from other papers | Cite them; check the licence (CC BY needs attribution); ask the authors for posters, ads and merch |
| Screenshots of simulators and tools (Gazebo, Unity, RViz) | Fine for explanation; label them "simulation" when they could be mistaken for hardware |
| Kent State archive (PhotoShelter) | University property; written permission first |
| Music in videos | Platform libraries or licensed tracks only |

## 9. Captions, credits and alt text

**Captions** say what, who (with consent) and where, in present tense, and any integrity label:
"[Robot or system] [does what] during [event or experiment], [Month Day], at Kent State University."
Add "(teleoperated)", "(autonomous)", "(simulation)" or "(2× speed)" where it applies. Never name minors.

**Credits:** lab-shot photos "Photo: ATR Lab, Kent State University" or "Photo: [Photographer name]"; UCM photos as
UCM specifies; third-party "Image: [Owner], used with permission" or "[Owner] / [licence]". In a deck, credits can
go in a small caption or on a final credits slide; in press kits every image carries caption, credit and alt text.

**Alt text** (required on every meaningful image: web, social, slides, PDFs, email):

- Formula: [who or what] + [doing what] + [where or context] + [the detail that makes the point]. One or two
  sentences; don't start with "image of"; repeat any text that appears in the image.
- Example pattern: "A student wearing a VR headset steers a telepresence robot across the lab while its camera view
  plays on a monitor." (Describe the actual photo; don't reuse this sentence.)
- Charts: give the takeaway in the alt text and the data in the text or a linked table.
- Decorative images (patterns, abstract backgrounds): `alt=""` or "Mark as decorative" in PowerPoint.
- ACM papers need `\Description{...}` on every non-decorative figure (see references/data-visualization.md).
- Concept illustrations: start with "Illustration:" so nobody mistakes them for photos.

**Web image sizes** Kent State uses: hero 1500 × 600, small feature 480 × 480, body images about 450 px on the long
side, profile 200 × 300, event banner 1500 × 550. Images should not contain text unless absolutely necessary. Export
JPG in sRGB for photos; keep originals. Print needs 300 ppi at final size (150-250 ppi for large posters).

## 10. Video basics and B-roll

**Capture**

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Landscape 16:9 by default (Kent State: "Videos must be shot in landscape orientation" unless the display is portrait). For Reels, Shorts and stories, shoot a separate vertical take or keep the action in the centre third |
| Resolution / frame rate | 1080p or 4K at 30 fps; 60 fps for robot motion you may slow down (then label the speed) |
| Shutter | About 1/(2 × fps): 1/60 s at 30 fps, 1/120 s at 60 fps. US mains is 60 Hz, so these speeds also stop lights and screens from banding |
| Stability | Tripod or gimbal; lock focus and exposure on phones |
| Audio | A lav or shotgun mic for any speech; record 30 s of room tone; deliver speech at −18 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP |
| Delivery | MP4, H.264, AAC 48 kHz; platform specs in references/social-media.md and references/web-and-digital.md |

**Framing**

- Interviews: subject on a third, looking just off-camera at the interviewer, eye level, a robot or lab softly
  behind, 1-2 m from the background; a navy lower third names them (references/graphic-elements.md, section 8).
- Demos: wide (operator, robot and space), then medium (the action), then close (gripper, sensor, screen). Hold each
  shot 5-10 s and film the whole task from start to finish, so you never have to fake a success in the edit.
- Keep graphics inside the 1920 × 1080 graphics-safe area (96 px sides, 54 px top and bottom) and above the
  caption zone.

**Integrity labels on screen or in the caption:** playback speed ("2× speed"); control mode (teleoperated,
autonomous or scripted replay); "simulation" for simulated footage (for pilot-training material, say which shots
are the real drone and which are the headset view or a simulated scene). No cuts that hide failed attempts inside
one "continuous" run.

**Accessibility:** captions on every video (review auto-captions; burn in for autoplay social plus a caption file);
narrate what matters visually ("the gripper closes on the cup"); nothing flashing more than 3 times per second.

**B-roll list** (shoot it at every major demo or event, 10 s or more per shot):

1. Building exterior and the lab door or sign (establishing).
2. Wide shot of the lab with people working.
3. Robot close-ups: gripper closing, wheels turning, lidar or cameras, joints moving.
4. Operator in VR with the robot's view on a monitor (over the shoulder), then the robot responding.
5. Hands typing launch commands or ROS topics (screen checked).
6. Assembly and soldering with PPE.
7. Robot point of view (camera mounted on the robot).
8. Simulation next to hardware doing the same task (labelled).
9. Whiteboard discussion (board checked or out of focus).
10. K-12 hands building and testing (releases checked).
11. Demo-day crowd: wide, then a candid visitor-and-host moment (identifiable visitors only with releases).
12. Time-lapse of a build or setup.
13. The ATR mark on a banner, badge or robot (for a closing shot; logo animation rules in references/graphic-elements.md).

## 11. Illustrations and the AI-imagery integrity rule

The lab has three **concept illustrations** (AI-generated in the brand palette, keyed to transparent PNG). They
explain ideas; they never show the lab.

| File | Concept | Good uses | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `assets/illustrations/illus-telepresence.png` (1467 × 642) | A remote operator in VR controls a distant robot arm over a network (telepresence, tele-embodiment) | Research-area explainers, intro slides, outreach talks | Caption below in slate or ink; ≥ 48 px clear around it |
| `assets/illustrations/illus-vr-drone-training.png` (1327 × 894) | VR drone-pilot training (the Immersed Pilot Training Simulator concept, a past project) | Explaining immersive training or teleoperation of aircraft | The virtual-view panel is top right; captions below or left. The drone is generic: never pair it with claims about a specific UAV product |
| `assets/illustrations/illus-k12-robot-build.png` (894 × 878) | Students building a small robot together (K-12 outreach) | Program pages and flyers when no consented photos exist | A floating cut-out (the whole table and all three students in frame): place it anywhere on white or mist with ≥ 48 px clear around it |

All three are transparent PNGs with a 24 px alpha margin, for **white or mist grounds only**. They are not for navy
fields at any size: 23-36 % of their pixels are within 35 RGB of navy `#003976`, so outlines, hair and clothing
merge into a navy field. On a navy slide, put the illustration on a mist `#F3F6FA` or white panel with ≥ 48 px of
padding (`assets/illustrations/README.md`).

Rules for using them:

- Caption them "Concept illustration" whenever they sit near real results; alt text starts with "Illustration:".
- Never in an evidence slot (quad-chart results, poster results, press photos), never captioned as the lab, its
  robots, its people or an experiment.
- Don't recolour, crop through the figures, add logos or text onto their objects, or collage them with real photos.
- No text over them. White or mist only; on a navy slide, put the illustration on a mist `#F3F6FA` or white panel
  with ≥ 48 px of padding. Never on photos or patterns.

**The AI-imagery integrity rule** (applies to every AI image, including new ones made with
references/ai-image-pipeline.md):

1. AI-generated images are only for icons, abstract backgrounds, textures and clearly **conceptual** illustration.
2. They are **never presented as real** lab photos, real robots, real people, real experiments, data or results,
   and never captioned as such.
3. Never use AI to depict real people (students, staff, the director), the lab's real robots, sponsors' hardware or
   any logo. Logos are placed from the official files; type is set in the brand fonts.
4. AI output contains no text, letters, numbers, logos or watermarks.
5. Every generated file keeps its prompt in a manifest next to it (`assets/illustrations/README.md` for the shipped
   set).
6. When in doubt, use a real photo, a diagram, or nothing.

The abstract backgrounds (`bg-*`, `social-*`, `poster-header-4x1.png`, virtual backgrounds and banners) are
covered in references/graphic-elements.md.

**Making a new one.** Generate in a working folder outside the skill with `scripts/codex_image.sh <out.png>
"<prompt>" [reference.png ...]` on a flat chroma-green field, key it, and keep the prompt manifest beside the file
(references/ai-image-pipeline.md, which has the prompts, the keying steps and the QA). New icons go through
`scripts/icons/generate.py`, which attaches the set's style anchor `scripts/icons/anchor.png` and calls
`scripts/codex_image.sh` by default from any working directory, and logs every prompt to `<out>/prompts.json`
(references/iconography.md §9). The release gate is references/qa-checklists.md §2.15.

## 12. Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] Real image of the real thing, or clearly labelled illustration or simulation.
- [ ] Releases on file for every identifiable person who needs one (event signage is not a release); no minor
      named; minors' faces only with a release.
- [ ] Location metadata stripped; no location tags on K-12 posts.
- [ ] PPE and e-stops right; nothing unsafe in frame.
- [ ] Screens, whiteboards, badges and sponsor hardware checked; embargo and invention-disclosure status checked.
- [ ] Licence and credit for anything the lab did not shoot.
- [ ] No edits that change what happened (tethers, operators, supports, nets untouched).
- [ ] Text on photos has a navy scrim that reaches 4.5:1 (70 %+ for white body text).
- [ ] Alt text written; caption carries speed, control-mode and simulation labels where relevant.
- [ ] Video captioned; no flashing above 3 per second; music licensed.
